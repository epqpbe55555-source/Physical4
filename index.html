01 · The essentials

Every law, in one scroll.

No lecture-length prose — just the definitions, formulas, and rules of thumb worth remembering.

Definitions

  • Quantitative: concentration of solute in a saturated solution at a given temperature.
  • Qualitative: spontaneous interaction of two+ substances forming a homogeneous molecular dispersion.
  • Saturated — solute in equilibrium with solid phase.
  • Unsaturated — below the saturation concentration.
  • Supersaturated — more solute than the limit predicts (metastable).

Factors Affecting Solubility

  • Physical & chemical properties of solute/solvent
  • Temperature
  • Pressure above the solution
  • pH of the solution
  • State of subdivision (particle size) of the solute

Solubility Expressions

  • Molarity, Normality, Molality, Mole fraction, % (w/w, w/v, v/v)
  • USP: mL of solvent needed to dissolve 1 g solute.

Solvent Classification

  • Polar (water, glycols, alcohols) — dissolve ionic & polar solutes via 4 mechanisms: high dielectric constant, dipole solvation, H-bonding, acid–base reaction.
  • Nonpolar (hydrocarbons) — dissolve nonpolar solutes via weak van der Waals forces only.
  • Semipolar (ketones) — act as intermediate solvents / cosolvents (e.g. acetone bridges ether & water).
  • Rule: "like dissolves like."

Gases in Liquids

C₂ = σp (Henry's Law)
  • ↑ Pressure → ↑ gas solubility
  • ↑ Temperature → ↓ gas solubility
  • Electrolytes/nonelectrolytes cause salting out

Liquids in Liquids

  • Ideal solution: obeys Raoult's law (adhesive = cohesive forces)
  • Negative deviation (adhesive ≫ cohesive) → ↑ solubility
  • Positive deviation (cohesive ≫ adhesive) → ↓ solubility
  • Complete miscibility: alcohol–water · Partial miscibility: phenol–water
  • Blending: a 3rd agent soluble in both raises mutual solubility

Ideal Solubility of Solids

−log X₂ⁱ = ΔHf/2.303R · (T₀−T)/(T·T₀)
  • Depends on temperature (↑T → ↑solubility), melting point (↑m.p → ↓solubility), and molar heat of fusion.
  • Ideal solubility is not affected by the nature of the solvent.
  • Not valid when T > T₀ (solute is a liquid then).

Non‑Ideal Solutions

a₂ = X₂γ₂  →  −log X₂ⁱ = ΔHf/2.303R·(T₀−T)/(T·T₀) + log γ₂
  • γ₂ = rational activity coefficient; γ₂ = 1 for an ideal solution.
  • X₂ and γ₂ are inversely related — the less "ideal" the fit, the bigger the correction.

Strong Electrolytes & Temperature

  • Endothermic dissolution → solubility rises with T (e.g. KBr)
  • Exothermic dissolution → solubility falls with T (e.g. cerium(III) sulfate)
  • NaCl: heat of solution ≈ 0 → solubility barely changes
  • Na₂SO₄·10H₂O → endothermic below ~32 °C, exothermic above it

Weak Electrolytes & pH

pH = pKa + log([A⁻]/[HA])
pHp = pKa + log[(S−S₀)/S₀] (acid)
pHp = pKw−pKb + log[S₀/(S−S₀)] (base)
  • pHp = pH below which an acid (or above which a base) precipitates.
  • Cosolvency: a mixture of solvents dissolves more than either alone (alcohol/glycerin + water).
  • Alcohol raises solubility of the un-ionized species, but also raises pKa (lowers dissociation).

Surfactants, Complexation & Particle Size

  • Surfactants solubilize weak acids/bases (micellar solubilization).
  • Complexation can raise solubility (I₂ + KI) or lower it (tetracycline + Ca²⁺ → insoluble).
  • Smaller particles → more surface area → higher solubility.

Slightly Soluble Electrolytes — Ksp

AgCl ⇌ Ag⁺ + Cl⁻  →  Ksp = [Ag⁺][Cl⁻]
  • Common ion → shifts equilibrium left → decreases solubility.
  • Non-common ion (moderate conc.) → lowers activity coefficient → increases solubility.

Distribution Law & Extraction

K = C₁ / C₂
Wₙ = W·(KV₁ / (KV₁+V₂))ⁿ
  • Valid at constant T, when the solute is the same species in both phases.
  • Extraction is most efficient with many small portions (large n, small V₂) rather than one big one.

Dissociation/Association & Preservatives

C = (Kq + 1 + Ka/[H₃O⁺])·[HA]w
  • Benzoic acid: monomer ⇌ dimer in oil; unionized ⇌ ionized in water.
  • Preservative action comes almost entirely from the undissociated acid — it crosses microbial membranes.
